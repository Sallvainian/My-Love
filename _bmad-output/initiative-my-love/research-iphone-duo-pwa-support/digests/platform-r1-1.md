- Inner display, portrait (one tab): width 669, SVH 867, LVH 902. | WK271 | WebKit | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | viewport-size | Table row: "Inner display, portrait (with one tab) 669 867 902"

### Q2: side controls and safe areas
- Premise correction: the side controls in these sources are system and app UI moved to the side (Dynamic Island, status bar, toolbar, tab bar, and in Safari its own controls), not physical hardware buttons. | HIG | Apple | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | safe-area | "Controls on the side include both system and app elements: the Dynamic Island, the status bar, the toolbar (including navigation buttons), and the tab bar."
- On the outer display in portrait, Safari puts these controls in a column on the right edge, full height. | WK271 (image alt text) | WebKit | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | medium (stated only in alt text; side named for portrait only) | safe-area | alt="… the outer (smaller) display in portrait. The webpage fills most of the screen, except for a column on the right. That space holds the controls for Safari and for the phone itself."
- iOS moves controls to the side because the outer display is wider and shorter than on other iPhones. | WK271 | WebKit | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | safe-area | "the outer display of iPhone Duo is wider and shorter than other iPhone displays. To give content more vertical space, iOS moves controls to the side."
- The controls stay on the side on the inner display in landscape. Only the inner display in portrait keeps horizontal bars. The side is tied to the hardware and the outer camera, and it does not flip in right-to-left languages. | HIG | Apple | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high (native apps; the HIG does not say how much of this applies to Safari or web apps) | safe-area | "The exception is the inner display in portrait, which has enough vertical space to keep standard horizontal bars." / "they hold the same position relative to the camera on the outer display, and stay on the same side in right-to-left languages."
- In Split View, each app puts its controls along its own outer edge. | HIG | Apple | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high (native) | safe-area | "When two apps share the inner display with Split View multitasking, each one places controls along its outer edge, so the left app has controls on the left."
- In Safari 27.1, web content does not extend behind the side controls. Safari fills that area with the page's background colour. | WK271 | WebKit | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | safe-area | "In Safari 27.1, web content does not currently extend to fill the space behind these controls. Just like on other platforms, Safari looks at the web page and extends its background color to fill the space."
- WebKit treats `viewport-fit="cover"` plus the env() insets as only a possible future way to open that space, and is looking at alternatives. The post never says that `viewport-fit=cover` works in 27.1. | WK271 | WebKit | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | medium (my reading: the "does not currently extend" sentence is unconditional, but the post never names `viewport-fit=cover` behaviour today) | safe-area | "We could use the HTML viewport meta tag with viewport-fit=\"cover\" to extend the webpage into that space … We are investigating if there's a better way to accomplish the same result … stay tuned."
- Apple's native guidance: lay out with safe areas so controls on either edge do not cover content. | HIG | Apple | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high (native) | safe-area | "Use safe areas to make sure controls don't cover your content, including controls on the opposite edge"

### Q3: fold and posture APIs
- Device Posture API (`navigator.devicePosture`, `device-posture` media query) ships in Safari 27.1 behind a feature flag. It is not enabled by default. | RN271 | Apple | 2026-10 (no date on the page; date inferred from the release) | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | api-availability | "Added support for the Device Posture API behind a feature flag, so websites can respond when a device is folded." (170005463)
- Viewport Segments API (`horizontal-viewport-segments` / `vertical-viewport-segments` media queries, six `env(viewport-segment-*)` variables) ships behind a feature flag. It is not enabled by default. | RN271 | Apple | 2026-10 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | api-availability | "Added support for the Viewport Segments API behind a feature flag, so websites can lay out content around a fold" (176426722)
- Both APIs are a developer preview on iPhone Duo only. Users must turn them on in Settings > Apps > Safari > Advanced > Feature Flags. | WK271 | WebKit | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | api-availability | "Both are supported in Safari 27.1 on iPhone Duo as a developer preview behind feature flags. To enable, go to Settings > Apps > Safari > Advanced > Feature Flags, and turn on both \"Device Posture API\" and \"Viewport Segments API\"."
- Both APIs may still change. | WK271 | WebKit | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | api-availability | "With the Device Posture API and the Viewport Segments API behind flags, there may still be time to evolve how they work."
- Posture values: `folded` means partially folded (book or laptop pose). `continuous` means flat, which covers both fully open and fully closed. Without a document, the type defaults to `continuous`. | WK271, RN271 | WebKit / Apple | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | api-availability | "continuous, when the screen is flat — which includes both fully open and fully closed displays"; RN: "Note that without a document, navigator.devicePosture.type defaults to continuous."
- The env() variables take a column index and then a row index, counted from 0 at the top left. `window.viewport.segments` returns `DOMRect[]`. WebKit recommends re-reading it on posture `change` and on `resize`. | WK271 | WebKit | 2026-10-23 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | api-availability | "Each takes two indexes: the segment's column, then its row, counting from 0 at the top left." / "window.viewport.segments gives you an array of DOMRect objects, one per segment."
- The only fold behaviour on by default is user-facing: Safari shows PDFs as two pages in book pose. | RN271 | Apple | 2026-10 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | other | "Added support for automatically showing PDFs as two pages side by side when a foldable device is partially folded into a book pose" (170353889)
- Neither source names any other fold-related media query or API for Safari 27.1. | WK271, RN271 | WebKit / Apple | 2026-10 | accessed 2026-10-29 | medium (absence in two pages, not an explicit statement) | api-availability | (absence)

## Not found
- **Q1: devicePixelRatio.** Not stated anywhere: I searched the WK271 text, the RN271 text, the HIG and "Preparing your app for iPhone Duo".
- **Q1: half-folded / partially folded viewport size.** The WK271 table has no row for the tent, book or laptop pose, and no segment dimensions are given. The release notes and HIG give no numbers either.
- **Q1: inner display, portrait split view.** WK271 describes split windows only "when the inner display is in landscape". No first-party page says whether a portrait split exists.
- **Q1: standalone (Home Screen) web app vs Safari tab.** UNVERIFIED. WK271 says every number is "in Safari 27.1" and tied to the tab bar, which a standalone app does not have. No first-party page I read mentions Home Screen web apps or `display: standalone` on iPhone Duo. I searched WK271, RN271, the HIG and the Preparing doc. Do not derive standalone sizes from the Safari SVH/LVH numbers.
- **Q2: width of the side-control column, and its side in outer landscape and in standalone mode.** No pixel or point width is given. Only the WK271 image alt names a side ("a column on the right"), and only for outer portrait. The HIG says the side is aligned with the hardware and does not name it.
- **Q2: values reported by `env(safe-area-inset-top|right|bottom|left)` in any iPhone Duo state.** Not stated in WK271, RN271 or the HIG. WK271 discusses safe-area env() only as a possible future approach.
- **Q2: what `viewport-fit=cover` does in 27.1.** Not stated directly (see the medium-confidence claim above). Standalone web apps are not discussed at all.
- **Q3: webkit.org/status/.** The static HTML holds no feature data (the page renders client-side), so I could not confirm the status entries for Device Posture or Viewport Segments. The bugs.webkit.org quicksearch for "device posture" returned no bugs.
