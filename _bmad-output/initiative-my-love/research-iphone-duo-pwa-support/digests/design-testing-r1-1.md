## Claims

### Q4: HIG navigation placement

- On the outer display the system puts toolbars and tab bars on the side, and they stay on the side when the device is opened in landscape | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "People interact with the outer display when the device is closed, and the system places toolbars and tab bars on the side to maximize the vertical space for content. The controls remain on the side when the device opens in landscape to ensure a consistent experience as people move between displays."
- Bars go on the side in every pose except the inner display in portrait, which keeps standard horizontal (top/bottom) bars | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "toolbars, tab bars, and navigation controls that are typically at the top and bottom of the display move to the side ... The exception is the inner display in portrait, which has enough vertical space to keep standard horizontal bars."
- The side column holds both system and app elements: the Dynamic Island, the status bar, the toolbar (including navigation buttons) and the tab bar | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "Controls on the side include both system and app elements: the Dynamic Island, the status bar, the toolbar (including navigation buttons), and the tab bar."
- In Split View multitasking on the inner display, each app puts its controls on its own outer edge | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "When two apps share the inner display with Split View multitasking, each one places controls along its outer edge, so the left app has controls on the left."
- The side controls are fixed to the hardware: they keep the same position relative to the outer camera and do not flip in right-to-left languages. The page names the camera as the hardware they align with; it does not mention side buttons | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "Because controls on the vertical axis stay aligned with the hardware, they hold the same position relative to the camera on the outer display, and stay on the same side in right-to-left languages." Also: "The outer front-facing camera is in the corner and is always visible, vertically aligned with controls on the side."
- The outer camera is an always-present reserved region that the system works around when controls are on the side | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "The outer front-facing camera. This region is always present, and expands into the Dynamic Island for Live Activities. When controls are on the side, the system automatically accounts for it and arranges elements accordingly."
- Do not override the default bar placement | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "In general, don't override the default bar placement. The position of controls on the vertical axis is one of the core patterns of iPhone Duo."
- Keep the controls' relative positions stable from pose to pose, because not every pose puts them on the side | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "Because not every pose places controls vertically on the side ... it's important to keep controls' relative positions as similar as possible so people don't have to relearn where actions live"
- Content space is asymmetric, so use safe areas to keep controls from covering content, including controls on the opposite edge in Split View | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "Use safe areas to make sure controls don't cover your content, including controls on the opposite edge"
- Order in the side column: primary navigation (Back/Close) at the top of the vertical axis, then prominent actions such as Done. Overflow goes from bottom to top | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "Reserve the top of the vertical axis for primary navigation controls, like Back or Close, followed by prominent actions, like Done." / "Items overflow from bottom to top by default."
- When space runs short, navigation-focused views keep the tab bar and move toolbar items into overflow (the default). Task-oriented views minimize the tab bar | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "In navigation-focused experiences, move toolbar items into the overflow menu so the tab bar and primary destinations remain accessible. This is the default bar compression behavior."
- Text-labelled buttons stay in a horizontal bar, so prefer symbols | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "Labels that include text stay in a horizontal bar, so prefer a symbol wherever one works."
- Interfaces that need no bars may span the full display width, provided nothing conflicts with the Dynamic Island or the status bar | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "Some layouts can span the full display ... as long as nothing conflicts with the Dynamic Island or the status bar."
- Sidebars and split views: a split view expands on the inner display and collapses to one pane on the outer display, and adjusts its column widths to the fold | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "a split view expands on the inner display and collapses to a single pane on the outer display ... adjusting width and margins to adapt to the fold."
- Half-folded posture: the fold is a conditional reserved region that splits the inner display. Keep important elements away from the centre. Use an even number of grid columns | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "When the device is partially open, the folding region divides the inner display into multiple usable regions, excluding the region at the center as the display folds." / "In a grid-style layout, prefer an even number of columns so content divides cleanly."
- Avoid big layout changes when the device folds. Make small adjustments instead | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "Move only what's necessary to keep elements visible and easy to tap ... favor small adjustments over rearrangement."
- Navigation containers go outside arrangement views | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "place navigation containers like navigation split views and tab views around it rather than within it."
- Size classes: compact width for the outer display, regular width for the inner display. No separate layout for each pose | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "A compact width layout for the outer display and a regular width layout for the inner display give you the fundamentals for every pose."
- The automatic side placement is described for apps that use standard system components | same URL | Apple (HIG) | 2026-09-09 | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "If you use standard system components, you receive this layout automatically, but you may want to refine it based on the needs of your app."
- Developer doc: bars move together to the side "in some poses". Review bars so frequent controls stay visible | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/preparing-your-app-for-iphone-duo | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-10-29 | high | design-guidance | "iPhone Duo presents navigation bars, toolbars, and tab bars together vertically on the side of the display in some poses."
- Apps built with Xcode 26 or earlier do not extend under the status bar and camera | same URL | Apple | undated | accessed 2026-10-29 | medium | version-compat | "When you build with Xcode 26 and earlier, your app doesn't extend under the status bar and camera." (Native-app statement. The page does not say how this applies to a Safari web app.)

## Not found

- **Q4, guidance for web apps / Home Screen web apps**: neither the HIG page nor the prep doc mentions Safari, web content or Home Screen web apps. Every placement rule is written for system bars ("If you use standard system components, you receive this layout automatically"). No first-party page read says whether a web app's own bottom dock should move to the side.
- **Q4, side hardware controls (buttons)**: the HIG page mentions only the outer camera and "aligned with the hardware". It says nothing about volume or power buttons, or about content near them. Searched the full HIG page text.
- **Q4, which side the bars go on** (left or right edge, per display and orientation): the HIG text says "on the side" and "along one edge", tied to the camera position, but names neither side in text. The images (e.g. `designing-for-iphone-safe-area-outer-camera.png`) were not examined.
- **Q4, inner display landscape vs portrait**: inner portrait keeps horizontal bars and inner landscape puts them on the side (both stated). Explicit guidance for the outer display in landscape was not found beyond "controls ... move to the side" generally.
- **Q5(a), simulating fold states, postures or display switching in the Simulator**: the Xcode 27.1 release notes do not describe Simulator controls for folding, posture or switching displays. The HIG points to Device Hub, which was not read.
- **Q5(a), Safari Web Inspector attaching to a Home Screen web app in the iPhone Duo Simulator**: not stated in the Xcode 27.1 or Safari 27.1 release notes. No webkit.org page was read this run (source budget).
- **Q5(a), whether the Safari 27.1 Device Posture and Viewport Segments feature flags can be enabled in the Simulator, or are on in installed web apps**: not stated.
- **Q5(b), Playwright fold, viewport-segment or posture emulation**: none found in descriptors, the emulation guide or the `emulateMedia` API (main branch). Not checked: Chromium CDP-level posture/segment overrides through `CDPSession`, and per-release notes for 1.60–1.64.
- **Q5(b), safe-area inset emulation and `display-mode: standalone` emulation**: no Playwright doc read mentions either. Absence is from grep of three doc files, not a statement by Playwright.
